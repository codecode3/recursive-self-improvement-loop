# Lens: what the human wanted

**Question:** what did the human want the finished thing to be, and where did the result fall short of it?

1. Rebuild the human's **intent** from their own words:
   - the setup form or first brief;
   - the creative edits;
   - reactions ("that is great", "no", "more …");
   - anything they asked for more than once.
   Cover the premise, the audience, the structure (story, flow, sections), the characters or components and the role of each, the tone, and the must-haves.
2. Compare it with **what shipped**. Use the last reports, final commits and the final render, upload or release described in the logs:
   - must-haves that never made it ("the scream was never sung");
   - parts that got far less weight than the human gave them ("allies got about 3 seconds");
   - choices made late that broke earlier work (a take swap three hours in that forced a re-sync);
   - known bugs that shipped.
3. Say **when** each gap could have been caught cheapest. Was it at the brief, the plan, the first cut, or before the final?
4. Propose what future agents should **lock or check early**. Examples:
   - a beat sheet or outline the human approves;
   - a must-have list checked against the output;
   - a form field;
   - a "known bugs block the release" rule.

Also report what the human clearly **liked**, so future agents keep doing it.

Example from "Slop": an artist's work is called slop, a vase painter with 15 years of craft and four allies, singing alone in a laundromat with cuts to the band and the story. The "SLOP. SLOP. SLOP?" scream was never sung, the chosen take had no final chorus, and the ally cameos were about 3 s each.
